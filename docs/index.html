🍌 Banana scale: the more bananas, the further left. 0 bananas = far right, 7 = far left. Based on each candidate's political family (far right → sovereignists → right → centre → social democrats → greens → left → far left), so it is an approximation, not a ranking of individuals. "Other" candidates have no place on the scale and get one big banana.

Notes. This list is provisional. Only candidates who gather 500 valid sponsorships and are validated by the Constitutional Council will appear on the ballot (12 did in 2022). Presidents are limited to two consecutive terms, so Emmanuel Macron cannot run. Within each group, candidates are ordered roughly from left to right; order carries no other meaning.

Links and avatars. Website links come from each candidate's France-Vote profile ("Site officiel"). Where that is a party or movement site rather than a personal one, the button says "Party site". "Find on X" opens an X people search for the name, because I could not verify each official handle from here. The round avatars show initials, not photos.

Age and sex. Birth dates come from each person's English Wikipedia page, and ages are calculated from today's date when the page loads. For 13 minor or little-documented candidates I could not find a reliable birth date, so no age is shown. Sex is shown as presented in public sources and, for those minor candidates, inferred from first names and the press's grammatical forms.

Children. Numbers are as stated on each person's English Wikipedia page. "children n/a" means the page doesn't say, not that the person has none. *Xavier Bertrand's page gives 3 in the text and 5 in the infobox.

World Popularity Index (🌍 WPI). A 0–100 score based only on the number of followers of a person's main account on X, on a logarithmic scale: WPI = 100 × log₁₀(followers ÷ 10,000) ÷ log₁₀(top account ÷ 10,000). Marine Le Pen has the most followers (about 3.05 million), so she sets 100. Followers come from qui-sera-president.fr, which counts them automatically once a day; the figures here are from 26 Oct 2026 and were not refreshed in the 3 Nov update because the source could not be reached. The index is not a measure of real popularity: it ignores other platforms, includes inactive or purchased followers, counts people who follow in order to criticise, and says nothing about voters. Followers worldwide are counted, not just French voters. It also doesn't cover all candidates: the source tracks 25 of the 45 on this page, and the rest show "n/a", which says nothing about their popularity.

Entrepreneurial Index (💼 EI). A 0–100 score for business experience, built from what each person's English Wikipedia page says. Points add up across categories and are capped at 100: founding a clearly commercial business = 55; founding a publication or venture whose legal or commercial form isn't stated = 35; an executive or management role in a private organisation (director, general manager, chair, editor-in-chief, manager) = 30; salaried private-sector work, including lawyers and journalists at private outlets = 15. Public-sector jobs, parties, unions, associations and elected office don't count. A 0 means nothing was documented on that page, not that the person has no business experience: Wikipedia is thin on this (for example, David Lisnard's page lists him as a "businessman" but names no company, and François Asselineau's page mentions a 1989–90 role at SADEF without saying whether it was a private company). The rubric is my own, so treat it as a rough guide and not a measurement of ability to govern.

Education (🎓). 🎓 = bachelor-level or vocational diploma; 🎓🎓 = one master-level degree or a Sciences Po / grande école graduate; 🎓🎓🎓 = ENA, or two or more master-level or postgraduate degrees. "Not stated" means the Wikipedia page names no degree, not that the person has none. Where the page names only a school (for example Sciences Po), I counted it as graduating and said so in the hover text. This is a rough scale of level, not of quality.

Years in politics (🏛️). One 🏛️ per started decade, counted from the earliest political role, candidacy or office documented on each person's Wikipedia page (hover for the role). "≥" means the page gives only a later date, so the real figure may be higher. Journalism, business or union careers are not counted as politics. Hollande's start (1979) is approximate.

Law and justice (handcuffs). One cuff = investigated or subject of a complaint only; two = formally charged or tried, including cases ending in acquittal or dismissal; three = at least one conviction. Everyone is presumed innocent unless convicted, and an investigation or complaint is not a finding of guilt. Only matters involving the person themselves are counted, as stated on their Wikipedia page, so cases where they are the victim or plaintiff, or that concern relatives or their party, are left out. "None documented" means none appeared in those pages, which may lag behind recent developments. A 'témoin assisté' status is counted as one cuff because it is not a formal charge. Please check current sources before relying on any entry.

Health notes (🦠). One 🦠 per health condition that the person has publicly disclosed or that major outlets have reported. "None documented" means nothing turned up in the pages I checked, not that the person is in good health. Candidates marked "not checked" are minor or late-added candidates with too little public documentation. Family members' health and speculation are excluded.

Update of 3 November 2026. New since 27 October: Gilles Platret (declared 8 Oct), Emmanuel Dubuisson and Jean-Michel Fauvergue (listed as declared), Olivier Becht and Mira Markovic details, and Édouard Philippe's new legal status (29 Oct). Still pending: Bruno Le Maire's announced October decision (nothing found), François Hollande's December decision, and the Écologistes members' vote on 10–13 December. The four candidacies without a verifiable declaration date (Mlekuz, Mathieu, Legendre, Durif) are kept but flagged. Gilles Platret was added late, so only his age and political start were checked.
